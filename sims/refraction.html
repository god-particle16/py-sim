What you are seeing

Light travels at its top speed in a vacuum. In any material it slows down, and the ratio of the two speeds is the material's refractive index n (n = c / v). When a ray crosses the boundary at an angle, one side of the beam slows down before the other, and the beam bends, like a car whose wheels hit mud on one side first. A ray entering a slower (higher n) material bends towards the normal. A ray entering a faster material bends away from it.

If light is moving from a higher-index material into a lower one and the angle is large enough, there is no refracted ray at all. Everything reflects. This is total internal reflection, and it is what keeps light inside optical fibres and makes diamonds sparkle.

Key formulas

n = c / v
Snell's law: n1 sinθ1 = n2 sinθ2
Critical angle (n1 > n2): sinθc = n2 / n1
Law of reflection: angle of reflection = angle of incidence

All angles are measured from the normal, the dashed line perpendicular to the surface, not from the surface itself.

Try this

  5. Set n1 = n2. What happens to the ray? Why?

Common mistakes

  • Measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
  • Thinking light always bends towards the normal. It depends on which material has the higher index.
  • Expecting total internal reflection when going from air into glass. It needs light to start in the denser material.

Check yourself

1. Light goes from air (n = 1) into glass (n = 1.5) at 30°. What is the refraction angle?

sinθ2 = (1 × 0.5)/1.5 = 0.333, so θ2 ≈ 19.5°.

2. What is the critical angle for water (1.33) to air?

sinθc = 1/1.33 = 0.752, so θc ≈ 48.8°.

3. Why does a straw in a glass of water look bent?

Light from the part under water refracts as it leaves the water, so your eye traces it back to a different position than the real one.
