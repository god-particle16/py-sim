What you are seeing

Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Three quantities tell the whole story: position (where you are), velocity (how fast position changes, with direction) and acceleration (how fast velocity changes). The three graphs are linked. The slope of the position graph is the velocity. The slope of the velocity graph is the acceleration. And going the other way, the area under the velocity graph is the change in position.

When acceleration is constant, the velocity graph is a straight line and the position graph is a parabola. If velocity and acceleration have opposite signs the car is slowing down; if they have the same sign it is speeding up.

Key formulas (constant acceleration)

v = v0 + a t
x = x0 + v0 t + ½ a t²
v² = v0² + 2 a (x - x0)
Average velocity = displacement / time

Common mistakes

  • Thinking zero velocity means zero acceleration. At the turning point the velocity is zero but the acceleration is not.
  • Mixing up speed and velocity. Speed has no direction; velocity does.
  • Treating a negative acceleration as always "slowing down". It slows the car only while the velocity is positive.

Check yourself

1. A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. How far does it go?

x = ½ a t² = 0.5 × 3 × 16 = 24 m.

2. What does the area under a velocity-time graph give?

The displacement (change in position).

3. A car moves at +4 m/s with a = -2 m/s². After how long does it reverse direction?

v = 0 when t = 4/2 = 2 s.
