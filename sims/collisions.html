What you are seeing

In every collision between two objects, the total momentum of the pair stays the same, as long as no outside force acts on them. That is the law of conservation of momentum. Kinetic energy is different. In an elastic collision it is conserved too. In an inelastic collision some of it is turned into heat, sound and deformation. In a perfectly inelastic collision the objects move together after impact and the largest possible amount of kinetic energy is lost.

The elasticity slider (the coefficient of restitution e) tells you how bouncy the impact is: e = 1 is perfectly elastic, e = 0 is perfectly inelastic.

Key formulas

Momentum p = m v
m1v1 + m2v2 = m1v1' + m2v2'
e = (v2' - v1') / (v1 - v2)
Elastic: v1' = ((m1 - m2) v1 + 2 m2 v2) / (m1 + m2)
Kinetic energy K = ½ m v²

Try this

  1. Use equal masses, e = 1, and an orange cart at rest. Watch the carts swap velocities.
  2. Make the blue cart much heavier. What happens to the orange cart? Now make it much lighter.
  3. Set e = 0. Do the carts stick together? How much kinetic energy is lost?
  4. Turn walls off and give the carts opposite velocities for a head-on collision.
  5. With e = 1, check that the momentum and kinetic energy readouts never change.

Common mistakes

  • Saying kinetic energy is always conserved. Only momentum is conserved in every collision.
  • Forgetting that velocity has a sign. Motion to the left is negative and must be included in the momentum sum.
  • Assuming "inelastic" means "no bounce at all". Only a perfectly inelastic collision makes the objects stick.

Check yourself

1. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits a 3 kg cart at rest and they stick together. What is their final speed?

Momentum before = 6 kg m/s. After: (2 + 3) v = 6, so v = 1.2 m/s.

2. In that collision, how much kinetic energy is lost?

Before: ½(2)(9) = 9 J. After: ½(5)(1.44) = 3.6 J. Loss = 5.4 J.

3. Why do heavy trucks cause more damage than bicycles at the same speed?

Their momentum and kinetic energy are far larger, so far more energy has to be absorbed in the collision.
