Work and Energy (Skate Park)

A skater rolls back and forth on a curved track. Watch energy change form between kinetic, potential and thermal. Drag the skater to a new starting height.

Controls

What you are seeing

Energy is never created or destroyed, only moved from one form to another. As the skater climbs, kinetic energy (energy of motion) turns into gravitational potential energy (stored energy due to height). As the skater drops, the reverse happens. With no friction the total of the two stays exactly constant, so the skater always returns to the same height. With friction, the track does negative work on the skater, removing kinetic energy and turning it into thermal energy, so each swing is a little lower.

Key formulas

Kinetic energy: K = ½ m v²
Gravitational potential energy: U = m g h
Conservation of energy: K + U + Ethermal = constant
Work: W = F d cosθ
Work-energy theorem: Wnet = ΔK
Speed from height drop: v = √(2 g Δh)

Try this

  1. With zero friction, check that total energy stays constant. Where is the kinetic bar largest?
  2. Change the mass. Does the skater's speed at the bottom change? Does the energy change?
  3. Move to the Moon. How do the energy and speed change for the same starting height?
  4. Turn on friction and watch the thermal bar grow. What happens to the skater's peak height?
  5. Use the hills track and find a start height from which the skater cannot clear the second hill.

Common mistakes

  • Saying energy is "lost" to friction. It is transferred to thermal energy, so the total is unchanged.
  • Thinking a heavier skater must reach a higher speed. In free sliding, mass cancels out of the speed.
  • Forgetting that potential energy depends on the chosen zero level. Only differences matter.

Check yourself

1. A 50 kg skater starts at rest 4 m above the bottom (no friction, Earth). What is the speed at the bottom?

v = √(2 × 9.81 × 4) ≈ 8.9 m/s.

2. What is the skater's potential energy there at the start?

U = m g h = 50 × 9.81 × 4 ≈ 1962 J.

3. With friction, where does the skater's energy end up?

It becomes thermal energy in the wheels and track, so the skater reaches lower heights over time.
