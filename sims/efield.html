What you are seeing

Every electric charge changes the space around it. We describe that change with the electric field, which tells you the force a small positive test charge would feel at each point. Field lines are a picture of this: they start on positive charges, end on negative charges, point in the direction a positive test charge would be pushed, and are closest together where the field is strongest. Field lines never cross.

The colours show the electric potential, the energy per unit charge. Red regions are at positive potential and blue regions at negative potential. A positive charge "rolls downhill" from red towards blue, just as a ball rolls down a hill.

Key formulas

Coulomb's law: F = k q1 q2 / r²
Field of a point charge: E = k q / r² (k = 8.99 × 10&sup9; N m² / C²)
Potential of a point charge: V = k q / r
Many charges: add the fields as vectors, add the potentials as numbers
Force on a charge in a field: F = q E

Common mistakes

  • Thinking field lines are paths that charges follow. They show the force direction, not the trajectory.
  • Adding potentials as vectors. Potential is a scalar, so just add the numbers, including signs.
  • Forgetting that the field of a negative charge points inward, towards the charge.

Check yourself

1. A 2 nC charge is 3 m away. What is the field there?

E = 8.99 × 10&sup9; × 2 × 10-9 / 9 ≈ 2.0 V/m (N/C).

2. What happens to the field strength if the distance doubles?

It falls to one quarter, because E is proportional to 1/r².

3. Why does the field between two parallel plates look uniform?

Near the middle of the plates, the sideways contributions cancel and the lines run straight and evenly spaced from one plate to the other.
