What you are seeing

A battery acts like a pump for electric charge: it pushes charge round the circuit, and the voltage tells you how hard it pushes. The resistors resist the flow. The yellow dots show the direction of conventional current (from the positive terminal round to the negative one); in a real wire the electrons actually drift the opposite way. Faster dots mean more current.

In a series circuit there is only one path, so the same current passes through every resistor and the battery voltage is shared between them. In a parallel circuit there are several paths, so each resistor sees the full battery voltage and the current splits between the branches, with more going through the smaller resistance.

Key formulas

Ohm's law: V = I R
Power: P = V I = I² R = V² / R
Series: Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3, same I, Vtotal = V1 + V2 + V3
Parallel: 1 / Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3, same V, Itotal = I1 + I2 + I3

Try this

  2. Increase R3 in series. What happens to the current through R1? Why?
  3. Switch to parallel. Compare the current in each branch. Which resistor carries the most?
  4. In parallel, is the total resistance bigger or smaller than the smallest resistor?
  5. Which wiring dissipates more total power for the same battery and resistors?

Common mistakes

  • Thinking current is "used up" by a resistor. The same current leaves a series resistor as enters it; it is energy that is transferred.
  • Adding resistors directly in parallel. You must add their reciprocals.
  • Believing a switch or bulb in parallel stops the others when it fails. Each branch works independently, which is why houses are wired in parallel.

Check yourself

1. A 12 V battery is connected to 20 Ω, 40 Ω and 60 Ω in series. What is the current?

Rtotal = 120 Ω, so I = 12 / 120 = 0.10 A.

2. Two 10 Ω resistors are in parallel. What is their combined resistance?

1/R = 1/10 + 1/10 = 1/5, so R = 5 Ω.

3. Why do Christmas lights wired in series all go out when one bulb fails?

There is only one path for the current. A broken bulb breaks the loop and the current stops everywhere.
