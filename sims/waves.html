What you are seeing

Waves do not bounce off each other. When two waves meet, their displacements simply add together. This is the principle of superposition. If a crest from one source arrives at the same moment as a crest from the other, they make a bigger crest: constructive interference. If a crest meets a trough, they cancel: destructive interference. Together these produce a fixed pattern of strong and quiet lines fanning out from the sources.

Whether a point is strong or quiet depends on the path difference, the difference between its distances to the two sources. The same idea explains the bright and dark fringes in Young's double-slit experiment with light, the dead spots between two loudspeakers and the way noise-cancelling headphones work.

Key formulas

v = f λ
T = 1 / f
Constructive (in-phase sources): path difference = n λ (n = 0, 1, 2 ...)
Destructive (in-phase sources): path difference = (n + ½) λ
Number of strong lines ≈ 2 × floor(d / λ) + 1

Try this

  1. Switch to one source and watch the circular wavefronts spread out. Measure the wavelength between crests.
  2. Switch back to two sources. Reduce the wavelength. What happens to the number of strong lines?
  3. Move the sources further apart. Does the pattern get finer or coarser?
  4. Set the phase difference to 180°. What happens to the line straight ahead between the sources?
  5. Change to the intensity view and count the strong lines. Compare with the readout.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the water moves along with the wave. Each bit of water mostly moves up and down in place; the pattern travels.
  • Saying destructive interference destroys energy. The energy is redistributed to the strong lines.
  • Forgetting that a phase difference between the sources shifts every line sideways.

Check yourself

1. A wave has frequency 2 Hz and wavelength 3 cm. What is its speed?

v = fλ = 2 × 3 = 6 cm/s.

2. A point is 7.5 cm from one in-phase source and 9.0 cm from the other, with λ = 1.5 cm. Strong or quiet?

The path difference is 1.5 cm, which is exactly one wavelength, so it is a strong (constructive) point.

3. What is the central line when the sources are in antiphase?

A quiet line, because every point on the middle line is equally far from both sources, but they are half a cycle apart.
